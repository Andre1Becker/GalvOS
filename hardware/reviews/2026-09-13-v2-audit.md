## Findings requiring design work

### 1. Laser shutdown is not independent of software

`J_ESTOP1.1` connects only to `U1.J2_17` (GPIO47); pin 2 goes to digital ground.
The watchdog reset is driven by GPIO38. Its output drives `R_SSRCTRL1` (330 ohm)
and `J_SSR1.1`. Neither E-stop nor scan output directly gates this path.
`src/safety/safety.cpp`, `allOk()`, returns the arming request directly when
`safety_override` is enabled; its input loop treats an open E-stop circuit as OK.
A broken E-stop cable therefore is not established as a hardware-safe state.
This is an observed interface constraint; firmware was not modified here.

Required decision: identify an external independent safety circuit or design an
on-board normally-closed interlock chain, power/reset supervision, qualified
watchdog, fault latch/manual re-arm and default-off hardware gate. Actual SSR
input current, switching polarity, leakage and power-disconnect wiring are
unknown. An SSR-control connector alone does not prove laser energy removal.

### 2. RGB pull-ups cannot establish fail-safe blanking

R27/R28/R29 are 10 kohm from +3V3 to the GPIO; R1/R2/R4 are 220 ohm in series
with each 6N137 LED to ground. With a floating GPIO, even an ideal zero-drop LED
could receive only `3.3 V / 10.22 kohm = 0.323 mA`. The Vishay 6N137 operating
conditions require at least 5 mA for a guaranteed input HIGH; typical LED drop
makes the pull-up current smaller still [3]. These pull-ups cannot guarantee
the optocoupler output LOW during reset. The output divider is 1 kohm to +3V3
and 1 kohm to ground, giving approximately 1.65 V unloaded when the optocoupler
is off; acceptance by the actual MN-1W5AT input remains unverified.

The input cathodes and output grounds are the same net, `5V GND Buck`, so the
optocouplers do not provide galvanic isolation in this circuit. Preserve the
firmware's inverted RGB polarity while designing an independently forced OFF
state, including MCU missing/unpowered, 3V3 missing, 5V present and USB-only.

### 3. DAC digital levels are outside a guaranteed interface margin

U2 AVDD receives filtered +5 V. SPI and CLR use ESP32/+3V3 levels. The DAC8562
requires `VIH >= 0.7 * AVDD`, hence 3.5 V at a nominal 5 V supply [1]. A 3.3 V
GPIO does not meet this guarantee. This must be corrected with a suitable
unidirectional level buffer and safe startup states, or a validated alternative
analog supply/output architecture. Lowering AVDD without checking the 0–5 V
DAC range and op-amp transfer function is not an equivalent fix.

### 4. Other ESP32 interfaces need level and cable protection

- U_SCAN1 operates at +5 V; its OUT connects directly to GPIO39. NE555 output
  swing is load-dependent and is not a guaranteed 3.3 V interface [2, 4].
- J_DMX1 supplies +5 V to the MAX485 module and connects RO directly to GPIO4.
  Determine the actual module circuit and provide a guaranteed 3.3 V receive
  signal; receiver-enable termination, bias, ESD and shielding belong in this
  interface review. The MAX485 manufacturer PDF could not be retrieved in this
  session; module-specific voltage limits are not claimed as verified.
- Fan PWM pins connect directly to GPIO16/17. Check fan-side pull-up voltage
  and use the required open-collector/open-drain interface for the actual fans.
- Long sensor and external control leads have no established ESD/transient
  protection strategy. SD module voltage compatibility and decoupling also
  depend on the exact module model.

### 5. The NE555 circuits are not qualified watchdog/scan safety circuits

U_WD1 has R_WD1 = 910 kohm and C_WD1 = 100 nF: nominal monostable time is
`1.1 * R * C = 100.1 ms`. U_SCAN1 uses 1.8 Mohm and 10 nF: nominal 19.8 ms.
Input edges only reach TRIG through AC coupling; there is no external transistor
to discharge/retrigger the timing capacitor as in TI's missing-pulse example.
The basic monostable cannot simply be assumed to retrigger continuously [2].

The watchdog trigger is pulled to +5 V and driven through a 1 nF capacitor by
3.3 V GPIO14. An ideal 3.3 V falling step leaves about 1.7 V, already above the
typical 5 V NE555 trigger threshold of 1.67 V, and above its 1.1 V minimum
threshold. Triggering is not guaranteed. Positive-edge overvoltage and reset
bias under all power sequences also need resolution.

The scan detector observes DAC command nodes through C_SCANX1/C_SCANY1, not
actual mirror position. It cannot detect a stalled mirror whose command keeps
changing. These two capacitors also couple the axes dynamically through their
common trigger node. Validate analog loading and replace or supplement this
function with suitable physical feedback/external scan protection.

### 6. Input protection and buck power budget remain open

J2 specifies 10–30 V; D2 provides series reverse-polarity protection. No input
fuse or TVS is present in the exported component list. Their ratings require
maximum supply energy/current, cable/transient conditions and ambient limits.
J4.1 and J6.1, labelled fan power inputs, connect directly to the buck +5 V
output and fan supply pins: applying another supply there can backfeed the
buck. Confirm whether they are intended outputs or separate fan supplies.

LMR33630ADDA is the 400 kHz variant. R_FB1/R_FB2 = 100k/24.9k set approximately
5.016 V. L1 is 6.8 uH; nominal COUT is 2 x 22 uF. TI's 400 kHz / 5 V example
uses 8 uH and 4 x 22 uF [5]. This difference alone does not prove instability;
it requires a design calculation using the actual load, capacitor effective
capacitance at bias, inductor saturation/DCR, loop behavior and thermal layout.
Do not substitute the 1.4 MHz example's capacitor recommendation blindly.

### 7. Analog range, grounding and supply sequencing need proof

Ignoring the 100 ohm series resistors, the 22k/10k stage gives
`Vout = 2.2 * (VREF - VDAC)`, approximately +/-5.5 V over a 0–5 V DAC range.
The series resistors, source/load impedance and resistor tolerances affect
gain and zero offset. Do not use the old statement about a firmware percentage
clamp as hardware proof of a +/-5 V output limit. DAC zero-scale power-on is
also not equivalent to centered galvos after a subtracting amplifier.

Both supply rails now have dedicated op-amp bypass capacitors, but bulk energy,
reference stability, output loading and missing-rail/back-power behavior remain
to be checked. The AGND/digital-ground split through R26 must be reconsidered
with SPI return paths, buck currents and the real PSU/chassis grounding; a
split plane is not automatically a low-noise design.

## Physical design and production gates

The existing PCB is not an acceptable starting release. Its fresh DRC includes
97 dangling-track, 69 silk-overlap, 48 solder-mask-bridge, 44 library-mismatch,
39 short, 13 clearance, 12 starved-thermal, 3 silk-over-copper and 2 outline
findings, in addition to 197 missing connections. Board/schematic parity is
not established. Existing Gerbers must not be ordered as a v2 release.

Before layout, confirm outline/mounting, connectors and pin-1 orientation,
module heights and actual DevKit variant/antenna keepout. Choose stack-up,
minimum copper/clearance/drill rules and current-rated power paths from the
real load and fabricator capability. Locate the buck's input loop, BOOT/VCC
capacitors, inductor and exposed-pad thermal vias as recommended by TI [5].
Place the new bypass capacitors at their named IC pins, not merely somewhere
on the same net. Reserve accessible test points for supply rails, reference,
DAC outputs, heartbeat, safety permit and shutdown outputs.

Release requires a matched PCB, freshly filled zones, clean ERC/parity/DRC,
reviewed assembly polarity/footprints, BOM with qualified part numbers and
ratings, fabrication/assembly files generated from that exact revision, and
documented bench tests. Begin with current-limited power and dummy loads;
measure rails, ripple, temperatures, DAC transfer, timing, cable faults and
power/CPU fault responses before connecting laser power. No bench measurement,
EMC/ESD test, optical safety validation or compliance certification is claimed.

## Reproduce the KiCad checks

Run from the repository root, using a temporary output directory:

```sh
kicad-cli sch erc --format json --severity-all --exit-code-violations \
  -o /tmp/galvos-v2-erc.json \
  'hardware/schematics/Laser Controllerv2_only_for_pcb_test.kicad_sch'
kicad-cli sch export netlist --format kicadxml \
  -o /tmp/galvos-v2-netlist.xml \
  'hardware/schematics/Laser Controllerv2_only_for_pcb_test.kicad_sch'
kicad-cli pcb drc --format json --severity-all --refill-zones \
  --exit-code-violations -o /tmp/galvos-legacy-drc.json \
  'hardware/schematics/Laser Controller.kicad_pcb'
```

The last command is intentionally expected to fail on the unchanged legacy PCB;
it does not save the refilled board. A future v2 board must have its own matching
basename and pass `--schematic-parity` as well.
