# V2.0.5 DMX receive input protection

Engineering checkpoint: `hw-v2.0.5-draft`, based on V2.0.4 commit
`61b6e558cb0eea339cc00c05baf04d4f877b6e2a`.
**NOT FOR FABRICATION OR LASER OPERATION.**

## Cause and bounded change

The 5 V-powered DMX module's RO pin previously drove ESP32 GPIO4 directly.
The exact module is not qualified, so a 3V3-compatible RO high level could not
be assumed. As for scan status, a noninverting SN74LVC1G17DBVR buffer now separates
the module output from the MCU voltage domain. This is not galvanic isolation.

## Electrical envelope and remaining qualification

Reference: Texas Instruments SN74LVC1G17 datasheet SCES351Y, revised October 2025:
<https://www.ti.com/lit/ds/symlink/sn74lvc1g17.pdf>.
Inspected reference SHA-256:
`c76fa723fac4502423967a1aa087dd669106b52b8de620c024b26e56f5d59309`.

- Recommended input range is 0 to 5.5 V, independent of its 3V3 supply.
  This does not permit 12.6 V or arbitrary cable transients.
- Ioff is at most 10 microamp at VCC = 0; input leakage at most 5 microamp.
  A 10 kohm, 1% pull-down bounds leakage-induced voltage to 50.5 mV.
  At 5.5 V it loads RO by at most 0.556 mA and dissipates about 3.06 mW.
- The 3.0 V VCC test row gives positive threshold 1.48 to 1.92 V and negative
  threshold 0.89 to 1.20 V. Do not extrapolate that row into a guarantee over
  every actual 3V3 supply/temperature corner. Qualify the module's RO levels,
  drive strength and waveform under the added load against the full datasheet.
- DBV pinout: 1 NC, 2 A, 3 GND, 4 Y, 5 VCC; noninverting Schmitt input.
  Nanosecond-scale propagation is small relative to a 4 microsecond DMX bit,
  but does not substitute for waveform/timing measurements.
- A pull-up to 3V3 could inject current into that rail from a 5 V RO signal;
  a pull-up to 5 V could backfeed an unpowered external module. The pull-down
  avoids those bias paths and gives LOW/break when disconnected, not UART idle HIGH.

The current receive-only firmware uses UART1/GPIO4 and rejects size <= 0 or
packets with an error. Only accepted packets update the DMX receive timestamp.
Holding RX LOW is not a valid DMX packet and is not an independent laser-off
command. Hardware timeout/safety behavior still requires physical verification.
The firmware's short-frame/start-code handling and its initial receive timestamp
also need separate review; this checkpoint makes no DMX parser-safety claim.

The exact MAX485 module circuit/MPN, RO voltage envelope, enable wiring, bus bias,
termination, isolation, cable shielding and ESD protection remain unqualified.
A MAX485 module is not proven equivalent to a particular manufacturer's IC merely
by its sales name. Attempts to retrieve the MAX485 manufacturer PDF did not
succeed; no new MAX485 datasheet guarantee is claimed here.

## PCB and preservation evidence

122 footprints, 1017 segments, 111 vias, three filled/named ground zones.
All 119 pre-existing footprint identities, positions and pad geometry match
V2.0.4. The sole old pad-net change is J_DMX1.3 to `/DMX_RO_5V`.
Of the 1098 original copper items, 1097 retain exact geometry: five of these
are renamed from GPIO4 to RO. The sixth renamed item, a B.Cu diagonal, is
trimmed from endpoint (121.763, 23.84) to (123.563, 25.64) mm.
Two original MCU-side GPIO4 segments remain unchanged.

The new output runs on F.Cu alongside the socket row and joins the retained
GPIO4 endpoint through a via. Local supply uses a short F.Cu connection to
the existing B.Cu 3V3 route. Ground connections are explicitly routed: the
router input omitted plane outlines to avoid falsely assuming a ground island
was connected. The actual PCB retains all three zones and was freshly refilled.
Unrelated router merging, rounding, widening and added copper were removed;
retained item UUIDs were restored. New copper is confined to the local stage
and its supply/ground tie-ins. No DRC rule, exclusion or severity was weakened.
Front-layer detail was rendered and inspected after routing.

## Release gates

This fixes the direct RO-to-GPIO voltage-domain connection, not the entire DMX
interface or laser safety. The prior independent shutdown/rearm, E-stop/override,
RGB reset-off, scan-monitor, fan PWM, power/current/thermal, analog range,
return-path/EMC, mechanical and physical-test gates remain open.
The user's two 22-position female strips remain specified; row spacing, exact
DevKit identity and antenna/USB keepouts still need physical confirmation.
KiBot's missing schematic/legacy PCB inputs remain diagnosed but unchanged;
switching those manufacturing inputs still awaits user approval.
